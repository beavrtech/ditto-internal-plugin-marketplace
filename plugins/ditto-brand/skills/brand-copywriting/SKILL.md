# Ditto Brand Copywriting

Owner: Flora Rivault. Changes to this skill are validated by her only.

Write Ditto copy that sounds unmistakably like Ditto: a sharp, warm expert who has done the compliance homework so the reader does not have to. This skill is the single source of truth for voice across LinkedIn, the website, emails, ads, product and UI copy, taglines, and the words on slides. The strategy is fixed; how punchy or measured you go flexes by surface (see TONE BY SURFACE).

## SOURCE OF TRUTH

These rules are distilled from Ditto's brand guidelines, the live site (trustditto.com), the Manifesto, and published posts. Do not hard-code facts that change (figures, targets, positioning). Pull them from the references below instead:

- Figures and proof points: Notion "Chiffres Ditto & marché" (https://app.notion.com/p/29926365f697802685a8ee22447d6077). This is the only source. If a figure is not there, mark it "[STAT: verify]".
- Target audience, personas, competitors: Notion "Positioning & Messaging", section "Target, personas and channels" (https://app.notion.com/p/3ce26365f697804daf4ae688bd64dd60). It evolves, so always read it rather than relying on a copy.
- Positioning and the one-line definition: Notion "Positioning & Messaging" (https://app.notion.com/p/3ce26365f697804daf4ae688bd64dd60). The one-liner below can change; the page prevails.

For any voice question not covered here, the tie-breaker is one test: would this line sit comfortably on the Ditto homepage or in the Manifesto? If not, rewrite.

## LANGUAGE

- French is the default. Address the reader with "vous". The Ditto page says "on" / "nous", never "je". Team members posting under their own name say "je".
- English is US English (organize, prioritize, color), second person "you", contractions yes.
- Both languages: no em dashes, short to medium sentences, one idea per sentence when explaining.
- French only: no comma before "et" ("A, B et C"), French quotation marks « », "une centaine" or "100+" for study samples (never an exact count unless verified).
- In English copy, say "sustainability" or "ESG" rather than "CSR".

## WHO WE'RE TALKING TO

Sustainability, ESG, quality-safety-environment (QHSE) and compliance managers, and the leaders who back them. Read the Positioning & Messaging page for the current definition of the target (company size, roles, segments). Often the sole or near-sole owner of the topic: capable, time-poor, juggling several frameworks, accountable to clients, buyers, investors, and auditors. Write to that person: credit their effort, respect their time, and tell them what to know and what to do next.

## BRAND FACTS (the canonical lines)

- One-line definition (FR): « Ditto, c'est une plateforme de conformité RSE et un coach pour vous accompagner. » EN: "Ditto is a sustainability compliance platform, and a coach to guide you." This may evolve: check the positioning doc.
- Mission: "We enable businesses to build trust and build better, together."
- Three beliefs (the lens): (1) Great partners make great businesses. (2) Compliance is an opportunity, not an obstacle. (3) When sustainable businesses win, the world wins.
- Differentiation: Ditto combines a platform, AI, and a dedicated human coach in a single solution, unlike point solutions or consulting firms, which do not empower companies to manage compliance efficiently over time. The human in the loop is the core differentiator.
- Ditto's people are **coaches**, never "consultants". The human is always the coach the client sees; AI works behind the scenes.
- What's real to reference: single source of truth for sustainability data; gap analysis; auto-fill of questionnaires and audits; policy and report drafting from the company's own data; supplier engagement; a dedicated coach plus proprietary per-framework methodology built in-house.
- Credibility markers and figures: never written here. Link to "Chiffres Ditto & marché".
- Sign-off / name-play: "Ready to get compliant? Ditto." (EN). French equivalents are to be validated before use.

## VOICE: THE FOUR CHARACTERISTICS

Ditto is Punchy, Positive, Expert, Approachable.

- PUNCHY (crisp, direct, pragmatic). Short and sweet. Prioritize what to know and what to do, with a touch of playful rebellion. "We get it. Compliance isn't everyone's cup of tea."
- POSITIVE (encouraging, joyful). Compliance as opportunity, never fear or guilt. Sage advice and encouragement that brings a little joy to a complex job.
- EXPERT (knowledgeable, confident, unpretentious). We've studied this across frameworks and around the world so customers do not have to. Confidence without pretension; correct mechanics, real specifics.
- APPROACHABLE (easygoing, down to earth, accessible). The opposite of an alphabet soup of acronyms and buzzwords. Talk like real people who get it, because we do.

Calibration: Expert + Approachable are the steady base everywhere. Punchy + Positive are the spice: heavier in heroes, headlines, social, and CTAs; lighter in detailed explanation, where Expert leads. Never let playfulness undercut the credibility a compliance brand depends on.

## HOW THE VOICE BEHAVES (signature moves)

These recurring devices are the most reliable way to make copy read as Ditto. Reach for them.

1. Affirm, then help. Credit the reader's work before offering yours. "You're already doing the work. Ditto helps you prove it."
2. Name the pain, then relieve it. "No more chasing scattered answers or outdated spreadsheets."
3. Short question, crisp answer. "Need to know when a policy changed? Just ask Ditto."
4. Triads and parallels. "Get compliant, stay compliant, and prove you're compliant."
5. Side by side. It is always "we" and "you": "with you every step of the way", "your coach", "your team's central command".
6. Earned playfulness. One vivid, self-aware turn per piece, not more: "alphabet soup of acronyms."
7. Credit, don't flatter. "The recognition you deserve." "The medal you deserve."
8. Concrete over abstract. Numbers and tangible verbs beat adjectives. Open on a real scene, a question asked in a meeting, a dated number, or an instruction that goes against habit.
9. Give the conclusion early, then the detail. Lists of three to five items that each stand alone.
10. Quote the customer word for word, with name and role, and thank them (only with their approval).

## TONE BY SURFACE

- Website / landing: confident and benefit-forward. Imperative headlines, one clear idea per section, a proof point or CTA close. The reference register.
- LinkedIn / social: the punchiest register, but Expert still leads for this audience. This skill covers the voice only. Formats, hooks, length, planning and checks live in the LinkedIn content skill.
- Email: warm and direct; a specific reason for writing up top; one ask; sign off in voice.
- Ads / short promo: one benefit, one verb, no throat-clearing.
- Product / UI microcopy: plain and reassuring; verbs over nouns; never cute at the user's expense; errors are calm and actionable.
- Slides: this skill governs the words; deck-designer governs design. Keep lines short enough to read on a screen.
- Long-form resource (Blog, News, Guide): NOT here. Hand off to resource-writer (same voice DNA, with its own bucket structures, SEO, and guardrails).

## VOCABULARY

To use:
- FR: professionnels RSE, responsable RSE, responsable QHSE, coach, accompagnement, conformité RSE, preuves (base de preuves), fiche d'évaluation, médaille, usage raisonné de l'IA, VS (ex-VSME).
- EN: sustainability professionals, sustainability manager, coach, support, compliance, proof base, scorecard, medal, reasoned use of AI.
- Refer to the product as "Ditto" (capital D, no "the Ditto"), a "platform" and "your central command".
- Capitalization: Ditto; EcoVadis, CDP, CSRD, VS, ISO 14001/45001/50001; ESG, ESRS, KPI. Define acronyms on first use in external copy.
- On-voice words and phrases: get/stay/prove compliant; structure; showcase; level up; audit-ready; every step of the way; build trust; build better; partners.

To avoid:
- "pros RSE", "consultants" (for Ditto's people), "copilote" / "copilot", "usage responsable de l'IA" (we say "usage raisonné").
- Anglicisms in French copy ("insights", "framework", "feedback", "overlap").
- Transformation verbs (booster, challenger, transformer, aligner), undefined acronym soup, stacked buzzwords, eco-platitudes ("save the planet"), fear or guilt framing, hype and superlatives you can't back.
- False promises: "no need to hire a sustainability expert", "without spending six months on it".

## GRAMMAR & MECHANICS

- Person: second person plus first person plural ("on" / "nous", "we" / "our") for Ditto. Copy is a relationship, not a broadcast.
- Em dashes: do not use them, anywhere, on any surface. This is a deliberate house rule. Use a period for a hard break, a comma for a soft aside, a colon to set up a list or payoff, or parentheses for a true aside.
- Numbers: spell out small numbers in flowing copy; figures are fine for stats and claims. Be consistent within a piece.
- Oxford comma in English. Exclamation marks are rare: let the verbs carry the energy.
- Hashtags: allowed, when they help. Never a stack.
- Emoji: allowed when useful and sparingly. Functional ones that structure or celebrate: 📌 1️⃣ → ⤵️ 👏 🥈 📊. Never decorative, none on the website.

## AVOID (AI TELLS)

Compliance copy that reads as machine-written undercuts a brand built on trust. Never use these words or phrases:

pivotal, vibrant, underscore, highlight (as a verb), tapestry, landscape (in the abstract sense), testament, foster, enhance, delve, garner, crucial, valuable, interplay, intricate, align with, resonate with, encompasses, groundbreaking, renowned, nestled, in the heart of, evolving landscape, indelible mark, deeply rooted, serves as, stands as, marking a pivotal moment, reflecting broader trends, contributing to the field, setting the stage for, in summary, in conclusion, overall.

Patterns to avoid (both languages):

- Do not replace "is" with "serves as", "stands as", or "boasts".
- No vague attributions ("experts say", "les études montrent", "plusieurs de nos clients nous ont raconté"). Name a real source or cut the claim.
- No closing line that turns the text into an aphorism, no recap conclusion ("en résumé").
- No trailing "-ing" significance claims ("emphasizing the importance of X").
- No "challenges and future outlook" formula.
- No sentence that explains why something matters without a specific, concrete reason.
- No mechanical "not X, but Y" or « Ce n'est pas X, c'est Y » flourish. A contrast that comes naturally is fine ("Ce profil n'a rien d'exceptionnel. Il a pourtant plus d'atouts qu'il ne le pense."). The established brand antithesis "an opportunity, not an obstacle" stays.
- Short sentences, fragments and real questions are fine when they carry data or emotion, especially in a hook. What to avoid is the same rhythm repeated sentence after sentence.

Brand exceptions, kept on purpose: "showcase" stays (it is a core Ditto word). Deliberate triads stay.

## HEADLINE & CTA PATTERNS

- Headlines lead with an imperative verb and a benefit, kept short: "Get compliant four times faster." "Answer questionnaires and RFPs automatically."
- Heroes can open by crediting the reader: "You're already doing the work. Ditto helps you prove it."
- Signature close (EN): "Ready to get compliant? Ditto." followed by a "Get Started" button.
- CTA verbs: Get Started; Learn more; Book a call / Talk to a sustainability expert.
- In posts, the call to action is a concrete action tied to the topic (compare with your own situation, run the test, audit what you have), and saving the post is the implicit goal.
- The bare "Ditto." as an affirmation is a brand asset. Use it for punch, sparingly.

## CLAIMS & COMPLIANCE (non-negotiable)

Ditto sells compliance and trust, so the copy must model the rigor it preaches.

- No greenwashing, no overpromising. Never guarantee scores, medals, or audit outcomes. Benefits follow from structured work.
- No invented stats. Use only figures from "Chiffres Ditto & marché"; mark anything unconfirmed "[STAT: verify]". EcoVadis publishes no score thresholds: never state a single cut-off, only dated Ditto estimates, as ranges.
- Never name a customer without their approval. Customer stories are not rewritten after publication.
- Stay humble about the product; where natural, acknowledge the non-product fallback rather than implying Ditto is the only possible path.
- Regulatory and framework claims must be accurate and current; if something is contested or recently changed (e.g. CSRD/Omnibus scope), present it fairly rather than asserting.

## EXAMPLES

Off-brand to on-brand:

- "Our platform leverages best-in-class AI to optimize your sustainability journey." becomes "Tired of chasing answers across a dozen spreadsheets? Ditto keeps your sustainability data in one place, so you can find it, prove it, and move on."
- "We guarantee you'll improve your EcoVadis score." becomes "Structure your approach, prepare properly, and go after the medal you deserve. We'll be with you every step of the way."
- "Click here to learn more about our solutions." becomes "See how Ditto works. Ready to get compliant? Ditto."

From published posts (anonymised, French):

- Hook on a real scene: « Il y a quelques semaines, il gérait les plannings d'une entreprise de transport. Aujourd'hui, il est responsable QSE, et l'évaluation EcoVadis fait partie du lot. »
- Hook on a question asked in a meeting: « Est-ce la moyenne des quatre piliers EcoVadis ? »
- Hook on an instruction that goes against habit: « EcoVadis autorise 55 nouveaux documents par évaluation. Mon conseil : ne cherchez pas à en déposer 55. »
- Customer story, warm and specific: « De 57/100 à Silver EcoVadis en un an : bravo au groupe [client] ! »
- Close on an action: « Enregistrez ce post et comparez avec votre propre base : quel document vous manque ? »
